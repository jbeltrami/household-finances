Lesson 01 · Data modelling

Freeze or Follow

You made this call correctly last week. This is the rule underneath it, so the next one isn't a coin flip.

The decision you already made

When you mark a Conta paid, the app writes a row into entries. That row copies the name and the amount from the template, and points at the Categoria. Two attributes, two different treatments, in the same insert.

That asymmetry looks like an oversight. It is the entire design. And the reason it is right generalises to every schema you will ever write.

The wrong question

The instinct is to ask “is this data duplicated?” — and then feel guilty about the copy, because normalisation says duplication is bad.

But that question mislabels the copy. Kenneth Downs makes the sharp version of this argument about order lines:

If the price changes 5 minutes after the customer places the order, they will expect to get the price as it was when they put it in the cart, and so you are actually doing the right thing by writing it to the order. Kenneth Downs, “Denormalization Patterns” (2008)

His point is stronger than “it's a justified exception.” He argues copying the price isn't denormalization at all. “The price this customer pays on this order” and “the current catalogue price” are two different facts that happen to have had the same value for a moment. Storing both isn't redundancy — it's storing two things.

The rule

Ask whether the event created a new fact or merely restated an existing one.

New fact → freeze it into the row.
Restated fact → follow it with a reference.

Apply it to your own insert. You paid R$470 on 10 March — that fact did not exist before the payment. Freeze. This bill is Moradia — that was already true of the template; the payment restated it. Follow.

Why getting it backwards hurts in two different ways

These fail asymmetrically, which is why one rule beats two instincts.

Followed when you should have frozen: the past rewrites itself. Change a template's amount and every historical payment silently reprints at the new number. Kimball is blunt about the stakes — in regulated environments, overwriting like this “may be outlawed”.

Froze when you should have followed: one thing becomes two. Rename Consumo to Alimentação and every row written before today keeps saying Consumo. Your report grows a second line for the same category, split at an arbitrary date, and no amount of squinting reconciles it.

Try it

Five calls. Answer before you read the feedback — the guess is what makes it stick, and being wrong here is cheaper than being wrong in a migration.

01An invoice records the VAT rate applied to it. The government raises VAT next year. Does the invoice row copy the rate, or reference the rate table?

Right. The rate in force on the invoice date is a fact about that invoice. Following would retroactively re-tax every historical document the day the law changed — which is the case Kimball says can be literally illegal.

Not this one. Tempting, because a rate table feels like the single source of truth. But “the rate that applied to this invoice” is a different fact from “the current rate.” Following rewrites tax history the day the law changes.

02A Receita points at a Pagador. You misspelled the employer's name when you created it and want to fix it. Does the Receita copy the name, or reference the Pagador?

Not this one. Freezing means the typo is now permanent on every Receita already written, and fixing it going forward forks one employer into two names that never reconcile in a report.

Right. The institution's name is one fact with one current answer. Nobody wants “the spelling at the time of payment.” This is exactly why income_entries carries payer_id and not a name string.

03An order shipped in March to an address the customer has since moved away from. Does the order row copy the address, or reference the customer's address?

Right. “Where this parcel went” is a fact created by the shipment. Following would make every past order claim it was delivered to an address that did not exist yet — and destroy your ability to answer a “where did you send it?” dispute.

Not this one. Try the tell: “the address at the time of shipping” is a phrase a support agent would genuinely use. That is the signal to freeze.

04A Categoria has a colour, shown as its bar in a spending report. You change Moradia from blue to teal. Do the historical rows copy the colour, or reference the Categoria?

Not this one. Freezing produces a chart where Moradia is blue until March and teal after — the one thing a colour is supposed to prevent. Presentation is never a fact about a transaction.

Right. Colour is a property of the Categoria, restated at render time. It follows, which is why categories.color exists at all rather than a colour column on every row.

05The hardest one. A recurring Conta's template says R$1.000. In March you overrode it and actually paid R$1.200. Does the March row copy the amount, or reference the template?

Right — and notice this one has to freeze even though nothing changed on the template. The override is the new fact. This is why the ledger materialises a row at all: a row in entries exists precisely when reality diverged from the template.

Not this one. Following would mean the March payment reports as R$1.000 — the amount you didn't pay. The whole reason that row was materialised is that reality diverged from the template, and a divergence is a new fact by definition.

The one that stays hard

Question 5 points at the real subtlety. The same column on the same table can want both treatments depending on which attribute you're looking at — amount freezes, category_id follows, and they sit side by side in one row.

So the rule isn't applied per table, or even per row. It's applied per attribute. Every column is its own decision.

Where this leaves a gap

Freeze and follow are the two ends. There is a middle you have not needed yet: versioning the source so rows can reference a specific version and get history and a single source of truth. That is Kimball's Type 2, and it is what you would reach for if you ever needed “what did the Moradia category mean in 2024?” It costs a lot more machinery. Worth knowing it exists; not worth building until something demands it.
